Which lever to pull

Machining time — 40%

This is the only line a redesign can move meaningfully. Three changes on this part would take it from 28 minutes to about 19:

  • Pocket depth from 15 mm to 12 mm — lets a larger cutter run, fewer passes.
  • Internal corner radius from R2 to R4 — bigger tool, higher feed.
  • Wall from 4 mm to 5 mm — less chatter, so the finish pass can run faster.

That is roughly $5.40 off the part before any negotiation.

Setup — 12%

Two operations mean two fixtures and two chances to lose alignment. If the part can be machined from one side with a dovetail fixture, setup drops and position error drops with it.

Material — 19%

Bar stock is not the place to save. The gains are in bringing the blank closer to net shape: a 25 mm plate cut down from 30 mm wastes 5 mm on every part, and that 5 mm is both material and machining time.

Finish — 13%

Anodising is a vendor trip. If the part is internal and non-visible, skipping it removes 13% and one queue. If it is visible, bead blast plus clear anodise is usually cheaper than a tighter machined finish that looks worse.

Quantity is not linear. Going 10 → 50 typically removes 30–40% from unit price because programming and setup amortise. Going 50 → 500 usually removes another 10–15%. Going 500 → 5,000 rarely changes anything unless the part moves to a different process entirely, in which case a casting or extrusion may beat machining outright.

When machining is the wrong process

Above roughly 2,000 pieces a year, the honest answer is often not "cheaper machining" but "stop machining". Extruded profile plus cut-and-drill, die casting, or investment casting each have break-even points that sit well below what most buyers assume. If a supplier never raises this, they are selling capacity, not solving your problem.
