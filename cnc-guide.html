Most CNC quotes come back expensive for one reason: the drawing asks for ±0.01 mm everywhere, on every feature, because nobody went back and decided which dimensions actually govern fit. This guide is the checklist we use before a drawing goes out to a shop.

1. What a tolerance actually costs

Tolerance is not a quality dial. It is a manufacturing process selector.

Loosening a feature from ±0.05 mm to ±0.1 mm usually changes nothing — both run on the same 3-axis mill with the same setup. Tightening from ±0.05 mm to ±0.01 mm changes everything: slower feeds, temperature-controlled room, more in-process gauging, possibly a grinder in the routing, and a higher scrap allowance baked into the price.

Indices are relative to an as-machined baseline on the same part. They move with geometry, material and quantity — a ±0.01 mm bore in aluminium is not the same commercial problem as one in 17-4PH.

The 80/20 of drawings: in most assemblies, two to four dimensions control fit and function. Everything else is clearance, cover, or cosmetic. Mark the critical ones, leave the rest at general tolerance, and the quote usually drops 15–30%.

2. Which tolerances to keep tight

Decide by function, not by habit.

Keep tight — these change how the assembly behaves

  • Bearing and bushing bores. Fit classes (H7/h6, H7/g6) exist for a reason; a bore 0.03 mm over nominal turns a press fit into a slip fit.
  • Sealing surfaces and O-ring grooves. Groove depth and side-wall finish govern compression. Get the depth wrong and no amount of torque saves it.
  • Datum-referenced hole positions in parts that mate with more than one component.
  • Any pair of features that set a centre distance for gears, pulleys or belt tension.

Leave loose — these rarely matter

  • Overall length of a bracket that is located by two bolts.
  • Fillet and chamfer sizes on non-sealing edges.
  • Pocket depth with 1 mm of clearance under the part it houses.
  • Cosmetic faces that nobody inspects with a straightedge.

Material is usually 20–45% of a machined part's price. Machinability moves the other half.

Two substitutions that quietly cut cost without touching function: 316L → 304 where the environment is not actually chloride-bearing, and 7075 → 6061 where the strength margin is already 3×.

4. The five cost drivers, in order of impact

01
Setup count

Every extra operation — 4th axis, second op, heat treat, plating — adds a setup. Reduce fixturing and the price falls faster than any negotiation.

02
Tolerance

See section 1. A single ±0.01 mm feature can add a grinding operation to the whole routing.

03
Material

20–45% of part price, plus scrap. Buying the bar stock yourself rarely saves money once cut loss is counted.

04
Quantity

Tooling and programming amortise. Going from 10 to 100 pieces often halves unit price; 100 to 1,000 rarely does.

05
Finish

Anodise, bead blast, electropolish, passivation — each is a separate vendor and a separate queue.

5. DFM checks that remove quotes before you send them

  • Pocket depth vs tool diameter. Anything deeper than 4× the tool diameter needs a smaller tool, a longer reach, and slower feeds. Cap pockets at 4× where you can.
  • Internal corners. A cutter leaves a radius equal to its own. Ask for R0.5 and you get a 1 mm cutter, which is fragile and slow. If the corner does not need to be sharp, say R3.
  • Wall thickness. Under 1 mm in aluminium or 1.5 mm in stainless, chatter and deflection start showing in the finish.
  • Threads to the bottom of a blind hole. Specify usable thread depth, not full depth. Otherwise you pay for a tap that bottoms out and breaks.
  • Surface finish callouts. Ra 3.2 is as-machined. Ra 1.6 needs a finishing pass. Ra 0.8 usually means grinding. Ask whether the surface is a seal, a slide, or a cover.

What to put on the drawing instead of blanket tolerances

A title block with a sensible general tolerance, plus explicit callouts only where they matter:

FieldRecommended default
General toleranceISO 2768-m (or -f for precision assemblies)
Critical featuresDimension with explicit ± and a datum reference frame
Surface finishRa 3.2 general; call Ra 1.6 only on sealing or sliding faces
Edge break0.2–0.5 mm, deburr all edges
Material certRequest mill cert only if the part is safety- or compliance-relevant
