A finish symbol is a
measurement method,
not a description.

"Ra 1.6" on a drawing is not a statement about how a part looks or feels. It is one statistical parameter taken along one or more traced lines, and it can be met by a surface that still leaks, still wears, or still fails a seal. Knowing what the number measures is how you stop paying for finish you do not need.

Ra and Rz are not two names for one thing

Ra is the arithmetic mean of the profile height deviations from the mean line, over the evaluation length. Rz is the average of the maximum peak-to-valley heights within each sampling length. One averages everything; the other is driven by the tallest features.

That difference is why two surfaces can share an Ra value and behave differently. A turned surface and a ground surface at the same Ra have different peak shapes: turning leaves a periodic thread-like profile, grinding leaves a finer, denser one. Under a lip seal, the turned one can pump oil along its lay; the ground one cannot.

The Rz band is a working relationship, not a conversion formula. The ratio between the two parameters changes with the process and with the cut conditions, which is exactly why a drawing should state which one it means rather than leaving the reader to assume.

Which process reaches which band

The practical question on a quotation is not "can you hit Ra 0.8" but "which operation gets there, and does the part have to be set up twice". This is the table a process planner actually uses.

Three places a finer note buys nothing

1. Non-functional faces. A clearance face that touches nothing but air does not need Ra 0.8. If the note sits on a general tolerance block instead of on specific faces, every surface on the part inherits it, and every surface gets inspected against it.

2. Surfaces that get coated afterwards. Anodising, powder coating and plating follow the substrate profile, but they also add their own. Specifying Ra 0.4 before a 60 µm powder coat is spending money on a surface that will be buried.

3. Sealing faces specified by amplitude alone. A radial lip seal needs the right Ra and the right lay direction. A plunging grind that meets Ra 0.4 but leaves a helical lead will still leak. The drawing should carry the lay symbol, not only the number.

How to write it so the quote is comparable

Put the symbol on the faces that function, leave the rest at the general block, and state the parameter. "Ra 1.6, traced across the lay" is a specification. A bare "smooth" is not, and a supplier quoting against it will guess — usually in the direction that costs you the seal.
